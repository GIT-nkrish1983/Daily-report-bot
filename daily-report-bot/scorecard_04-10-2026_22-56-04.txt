Gill collects the trophy and joins his team-mates. They pose with the WINNERS board. India won the first two matches convincingly with their top three batters doing the bulk of the scoring. Today, they lost Gill and Kohli cheaply. Rohit stepped up and missed out on another ton by 8 runs, Ruturaj Gaikwad scored 57 after being dropped four times but it was KL Rahul's unbeaten 129 that powered India to 351. In reply, captain Shai Hope opened the innings today and batted till the end to take his team over the winning line with a magnificent 162*, stitching four 50+ stands. Three matches were played in different parts of the country but the pattern remained similar. Good batting wickets, high-scoring encounters, centurions for both teams and the chasing team emerging victorious. It's time to bring the curtains down on this ODI series. The action now shifts to the shortest format. The 5-match T20I series commences on Tuesday (October 6th) and we'll be bringing you all the action. Until then, goodbye...
How moving down helped Shai Hope move forward
No visiting batter averages more in Asia than Hope, whose switch down the order has been central towards West Indies' improved middle-order numbers
Shubman Gill | India captain and Player of the Series: Lot of boxes ticked. I think there are still some areas that definitely need some fine tuning. I was very happy with the total. I think the game was in control. But being behind four overs with this dew and then in the last, I think they took the game away from us. Had we not been shortened the overs, the game might have went to the last over. [On whether there was a temptation to bring Kuldeep Yadav into the attack earlier] No, not really. We didn't know if Gurnoor was gonna come back and bowl with his cramps. So there was a bit uncertainty there. And we knew the match is gonna go till the end. And, he's a wicket-taker and I wanted his couple of overs to be there in the end. [On India's progress as an ODI side despite injuries and an unsettled squad] Decently pleased I would say, but like I said, I think there are still some areas. Our fielding could be much better. Overall in the series I think we dropped around six or seven catches, which is not a good sign leading up to the World Cup. You want your fielding to be top notch. [On the areas that still need fine tuning] Death bowling as well. I feel, sometimes we are not really clear whether we are attacking or we are defending, especially when we are bowling the death overs. One day such a game, if the opposition is five or six down, you are never really sure what fields to take, whether we are going for the death too early or we are still bowling kind of like middle overs. I think these are some areas which definitely we need to be better at. [On missing out on a big score after getting out early] You're always disappointed whenever you get out early. But I think today, I don't think I could have done much that I would have done on that ball.
Hope (2/2): [On West Indies' record run chase and the overall ODI series] I think we played some really good cricket in patches. The batters were scoring some runs. That's what we really wanted to do. So yeah, we've been doing really well for the last two, three years where batters have been more consistent. We asked them to put big scores on the board. It's always difficult bowling in these conditions. We know India, more batter friendly. You got to bring your skills out and very, very, very small margins for error. So from a bowling standpoint, we learned a lot. I'm sure the guys are going to take this and hopefully we can get a lot better in the bowling side of things going forward. [On recovering in time for the upcoming shorter format] So hopefully, I got two days to turn things around, so hopefully I'm match ready.
Shai Hope | West Indies captain and Player of the Match(1/2): [On winning the match while chasing and finishing the series on a strong note] Yeah, it's something in India, it's always a lot easier, especially when dew comes out. But yeah, we wanted to finish the series on a strong note, so I'm just happy the guys got over the line today. [On when he started feeling discomfortable] Probably about 30 overs in, felt a little something in the groin area. So just tried to fight to the end there. [On how he paced his innings and managed the chase] Well, firstly, we were a batter down, probably two if you count Vitel Lawes as well. So it would always be a challenge. And something that I had a great chat with the great man MS Dhoni, he was telling me you always have more time than you think. So just trying not to focus too much on the end result. Just look at the process, try to break the game down as best as I can and find ways to get the boundaries in the back end. I even thought about the overs that the Indian team were behind. So I knew they would have to bring a couple fielders in, in the back end. So it's always tough when you're trying to defend a score and you got to have five men in the ring. So all those things I calculated when I was batting. Just so happened that everything worked out for us in the back end.

Cricbuzz Live: Hope helps West Indies win 3rd ODI | India seal series 2-1
17:30 GMT, 23:00 IST: Record run-chase for West Indies, thanks to Shai Hope's incredible knock. In reply to India's 357, they lost the in-form John Campbell cheaply, with Siraj striking in the powerplay. That brought the other two centurions from the previous game together. Although there were a few plays and misses in the first powerplay, Shai Hope and Amir Jangoo batted at a good tempo and forged a century stand for the second wicket. Just when it looked like India ran out of ideas, a run-out resulted in Jangoo getting out. Hope was involved in two back-to-back fifty stands after that. First with Sherfane Rutherford, who was wrongly adjudged caught behind even though he reviewed. And then with Keemo Paul. That was a key partnership. Hope was struggling a bit and Paul became the aggressor to keep the asking rate under check. West Indies, who were ahead of the rate till the 40th over, lost a bit of momentum after Paul's downfall. With 56 needed off the last 5 overs, Hope once again pressed on the accelerator. He went after Kuldeep in the 46th over with a six and a four, hit back-to-back boundaries against Brar in the following over and then the 48th over almost sealed the deal for West Indies as Anshul Kamboj leaked 22 runs. Motie played his part as well as the unbeaten 62-run stand for the sixth wicket took WI home with 10 balls to spare.
